<objective>
Stop the two server builders from returning `requestState` AEAD key material to the allocator
unscrubbed.

Purpose: T-113-05 made the codec zeroize every copy of the key it holds — `RequestStateCodec`
scrubs its decoded buffers, its rejected rotation attempts, and its locals. The builders that
FEED it do not. `ServerCoreBuilder` (`src/server/builder.rs:100`) holds `Option<[u8;32]>` plus
a `Vec<[u8;32]>` of rotated-out keys and drops both in the clear; `ServerBuilder`
(`src/server/mod.rs:2652`) has the identical field; and `resolve_codec_at_build` takes the key
BY VALUE, so the very act of calling it makes another unscrubbed copy. The module's whole
reason to exist is protecting that key, and the first thing on its path leaks it.

Output: a crate-internal zeroizing key type, both builders holding it, by-value setter
parameters scrubbed after transfer, `resolve_codec_at_build` taking the key by reference, and
tests proving the scrub — with the public signatures and the semver posture unchanged.
</objective>

WHY NOT A `Drop` IMPL (the deferred item's own open question, decided here): a `Drop` impl on
the builder is incompatible with the by-value `mut self` chaining idiom — with `Drop`, every
`self` field move out of the struct becomes an error (E0509), so `build()` could no longer
destructure the builder. The `Zeroizing` newtype puts the destructor on the FIELD instead of
the struct, which scrubs on drop, survives moves, and needs no change to the chaining style.
Implement the newtype approach.

THE THREE COPIES that must each be handled — enumerate them in code comments so a later
reader can check the set is complete:
  1. the builder FIELD (both builders) — solved by the zeroizing field type;
  2. the SETTER's by-value parameter (`key: [u8; 32]` is `Copy`, so moving it into the wrapper
     leaves the parameter's own stack slot intact) — scrub the parameter explicitly after the
     transfer, exactly as `request_state.rs` already does for its locals;
  3. `resolve_codec_at_build`'s by-value `key` parameter — solved by taking a reference.
