## The three residuals, each with a mechanism, a reachable shape, a reason for being open, and an OWNER

All three carry the same owner: **the client-transport hardening plan named in this phase's `deferred-items.md` Disposition section — the same owner as entry 23 (CR-03).**

1. **The non-caching `AuthProvider`** (`WINDOWS.md` entry 26, from `118.2-25`). "Exactly one vend" is **preconditioned** on the provider caching what `get_access_token` vends; the trait imposes no such contract (`on_unauthorized` defaults to a no-op, `get_access_token` is unconstrained). Against a non-caching provider the generation check is **inert** — the loser skips the purge but vends anyway — so the lock converts two simultaneous vends into two **serialised** vends, which a rotating refresh token still rejects. **Reachable shape:** a durable agent holding one transport across concurrent tasks. **Unfenceable in-repo, not merely unfenced:** verified at this HEAD — `grep` over `src/` and `tests/` finds the unrelated server-side trait (`src/server/auth/traits.rs:450`) and, for the client-side trait (`src/shared/streamable_http.rs:4290`), **only test doubles**: `CountingProvider`, `MinimalProvider`, `DualTokenProvider` and `CachingProbe` under `#[cfg(all(test, …))]`, plus `CountingProvider`, `TestAuthProvider`, `MockAuthProvider` and `CachingAuthProvider` in `tests/`. **Why the assumption is nonetheless well-founded**, also verified at HEAD: the trait's own rustdoc tells implementors to evict "so that the subsequent `get_access_token()` call … returns a freshly-vended token rather than the now-invalid cached one" — wording that only makes sense if `get_access_token` normally serves from cache. **Durable fix:** a caching contract stated on the trait — a decision with an external-implementor cost, not a gap-closure edit.
2. **`PooledTransport`** (entry 27, from `118.2-23`). It inherits `Transport::shared_sender`'s default `None` **by explicit decision** with a `// Why:` at the impl (`src/shared/connection_pool.rs:621`), because the pool picks a connection **per send** and a per-connection worker owns the I/O, so no inner handle can speak for a future send. Consequence: a pooled `StreamableHttpTransport` keeps the exclusive `&mut` path. Narrower than the wedge — `send_to_connection` awaits an mpsc push, not the peer's response head — but **not zero**: with the connection's channel full (capacity 100) the push blocks on a worker that is itself blocked on the stalled peer.
3. **`Client::open_event_stream`** (entry 27; **found during `118.2-23`, after this plan was written**). It still holds a **READ** guard across the `subscriptions/listen` POST's response head, and a read guard is no better than a write guard there — tokio's `RwLock` is fair, so a writer arriving parks and every later reader parks behind it. Not closed because the owned-handle seam (`Transport::shared_sender`) does not exist on `EventStreamTransport`, and adding it is a **public-trait change**. **Blast radius: callers of `subscriptions/listen` only.** Verified in place at `src/client/mod.rs:5319` under the marker `KNOWN RESIDUAL (Phase 118.2, plan 23)`.

### Auto-fixed Issues

- **Issue:** `tests/windows_disclosure_tripwire.rs` is **not** in the plan's `files_modified`, but the constant's own rustdoc states the maintenance contract: "raise this floor when entries are added, never lower it to make a red go green," and explains that `!marked.is_empty()` is too weak precisely because entries can lose their sentinel through an in-place rewrite — which entries in this ledger document happening.
