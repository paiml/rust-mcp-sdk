## The `PooledTransport` decision, and why

`PooledTransport<T>` (`src/shared/connection_pool.rs:643`) is the ONE wrapping `impl Transport for ...` in the tree — the one place a new defaulted method can be inherited with no compile error and no fence.

**Decision: it KEEPS the default `None`, explicitly, with a `// Why:` at the impl.**

The reason is structural rather than incidental. `PooledTransport::send` calls `ConnectionPool::send_message`, which calls `get_connection()` — a load-balancing choice made **per send** — and then hands the message to that connection's mpsc channel. A per-connection worker task, owning its own clone of the inner transport, performs the actual I/O (`connection_pool.rs:204-212`). There is therefore no inner transport whose handle would be correct for a *future* send, and forwarding one would silently pin every shared send to whichever connection happened to be chosen when the handle was taken — defeating the pool's whole purpose.

**Named residual, for plan 24's ledger rather than left implicit.** A consumer who pools a `StreamableHttpTransport` keeps the exclusive `&mut` path. That is materially narrower than the wedge this plan closes: `send_to_connection` awaits an **mpsc push**, not the peer's response head, so a stalled peer does not hold the consumer's transport guard through this wrapper. It is not zero — with the connection's channel full (capacity 100) the push blocks until the worker drains, and the worker is itself blocked on the stalled peer, so sustained traffic against a silent peer can still serialise a client over a pooled transport.

## What the failure mode IS after this plan — with both accepted residuals named

A peer that accepts a POST and never writes its response head still leaves **its own call waiting, indefinitely**. That is unchanged and deliberate: pmcp has no default request timeout, a response head from a JSON-answering or Lambda-hosted server arrives only when the handler finishes, and any finite deadline here would fail a legitimate long `tools/call` that works today. What changed is *who else* is affected: nothing else on that `Client` is. A second call, a notification, a cancellation and `Transport::close` all acquire the transport and complete while the stall is in progress — measured, not argued, by fence 30. Transports that answer `None` from `shared_sender` — every transport except `StreamableHttpTransport`, including every external implementor, without editing a line — take the identical exclusive route they take today, and the one transport that opts in performs the identical POST with identical headers, identical 401 recovery and identical synchronous error propagation.

**Two residuals are ACCEPTED and must be booked by plan 24 rather than discovered by it.** First, *concurrent POSTs*: with the guard released, two POSTs can now be in flight on one transport at once. Three things a POST touches beyond its own call were checked by name — the per-caller POST-reader accounting (an atomic), the transport-wide `AuthProvider` (single-flighted by plan 25's `refresh_lock` from the purge **through the vend**), and `start_sse` reached from `post_body`'s 202 branch (made indivisible by plan 25's `restart_lock`) — and nothing beyond those three is asserted. Second, *outbound ordering*: the client no longer imposes a total order on outbound frames for this transport. HTTP never guaranteed one across separate POSTs, and transports on the exclusive path keep their total order; this is disclosed rather than buried.

**A THIRD residual was FOUND during this plan and is NOT closed — see Deviations, finding 2.** `Client::open_event_stream` still holds a READ guard across the `subscriptions/listen` POST's response head. Its blast radius is callers of `subscriptions/listen` only.

**2. [FINDING — residual left OPEN, needs an owner] `Client::open_event_stream` still holds a guard across an HTTP round trip**

- **Found during:** Task 3, while auditing the plan's must-have "no transport-guard acquisition anywhere in `src/client/mod.rs` spans an HTTP round trip".
- **Issue:** that must-have is **not fully true** after this plan, and saying otherwise would be exactly the unverified claim of record this phase has been burned by. `Client::open_event_stream` takes `self.transport.read()` and awaits `transport.open_event_stream(body)` inside it; that call reaches `post_streaming`, which awaits the peer's response head. A read guard is no better than a write guard here — tokio's `RwLock` is fair, so a writer arriving meanwhile parks and every later reader parks behind it — so a peer that accepts the `subscriptions/listen` POST and never answers still serialises the client, exactly as `dispatch_request` did.
- **Why it was NOT fixed:** the seam that closes it does not exist. `EventStreamTransport::open_event_stream` is reached through the generic `T` with no way to own one; closing it needs either a new accessor on that public trait or a `Clone` bound on `T` (breaking). That is an architectural change — deviation Rule 4 — and belongs to a decision, not to a gap-closure round. Adding it under this plan would also have put a second, unfenced public-API change into a round whose semver claim is "one defaulted method plus one trait".
- **What was done instead:** the residual is recorded IN PLACE, at the guard, with its mechanism, its reason for being open and its blast radius (callers of `subscriptions/listen` only; every other client-side send is off the guard). It is stated above in "what the failure mode IS" so plan 24 books it rather than discovers it.
- **Files modified:** `src/client/mod.rs` (comment only) — **Commit:** `05e3ad26`
