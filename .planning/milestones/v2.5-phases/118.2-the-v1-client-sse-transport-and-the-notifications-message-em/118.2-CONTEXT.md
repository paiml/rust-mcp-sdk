# Phase 118.2: The v1 client SSE transport and the `notifications/message` emitter - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Close the two residuals Phase 118.1 measured and signed off as **OPEN** sub-items of G-3 at plan
118.1-13's D-10 gate. Both are missing surfaces at opposite ends of the server-to-client channel
118.1 built and proved — the channel itself is **not** in scope.

**What is already proven and must not be rebuilt:** `binary(http_peer_roundtrip)` is 12 tests /
12 passed, driving `sample`, `list_roots` and `elicit` to completion over v1 HTTP. Its client is a
raw TCP reader that *does* hold the stream open. That contrast is the whole localisation argument:
the server half works; pmcp's own client cannot hold the stream, and no handler-facing log emitter
exists.

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### The client SSE read

- **D-01: Both whole-body collect sites become incremental reads.** The GET stream
  (`src/shared/streamable_http.rs:1002`) *and* the POST-response SSE (`:1543`). The roadmap names
  only the GET, but the POST case is the more consequential of the two: in StreamableHTTP a POST
  that answers `text/event-stream` stays open and can carry notifications **and server-to-client
  requests** before the response frame. Collecting it whole means progress arrives only after the
  call ends, and **in-tool elicitation over a POST stream deadlocks outright** — the client cannot
  answer a request it has not parsed yet.
  - The parser is not the missing piece. `SseParser::feed(&mut self, data: &str)`
    (`src/shared/sse_parser.rs:382`) is already incremental and already hardened. What is missing
    is a streaming read feeding it.

- **D-02: The collected-body cap becomes the parser bound.** Build the streaming parser with
  `SseParser::with_max_buffer_size(self.max_collected_body_bytes)` — 16 MiB default
  (`DEFAULT_MAX_COLLECTED_BODY_BYTES`, `:454`), already user-overridable through
  `with_max_collected_body_bytes`. **No new public config.**
  - *Why this is safe:* the parser bound is already the product of Phase 113's gap-closure round —
    latching overflow that survives `reset`, a bound over retained-state-plus-chunk with two
    independently sufficient enforcement points (review CR-01/CR-02, T-113-86). Its own rustdoc
    already prescribes the response: *"end the stream with an error naming the limit, not to keep
    parsing."*
  - *Known interaction, from the parser's own source:* the bound is evaluated over the chunk
    **total**, so "a chunk carrying many individually small COMPLETE events is refused whenever the
    chunk TOTAL exceeds the limit … behaviour depends partly on how the transport frames its
    reads." At 16 MiB this is not reachable by real MCP frames, but a planner lowering the bound
    must know the coupling exists.
  - *Rejected:* a separate `max_sse_buffer_bytes` knob (a second number users must reason about);
    a lifetime byte budget (a v1 session stream is meant to live as long as the session, so any
    lifetime ceiling is a timer on legitimate use).

- **D-03: Bounded auto-reconnect with `Last-Event-ID`.** The parser already tracks
  `last_event_id()`, and `apply_resumption_header` already exists.
  - *Why not "surface and stop":* real deployments idle-drop long-lived streams routinely (ALB
    ~60s, Cloudflare, API gateways). Without reconnect this phase's fix silently regresses to
    `NO_LIVE_STREAM` the first time a proxy blinks — the exact symptom it exists to remove.
  - **⚠ Constraint the planner must resolve:** `apply_resumption_header` is
    `#[cfg(feature = "v1-compat")]`, and it is *the only `#[cfg]` at a call site in that file* (the
    file's own comment says so and asks that a second one not accumulate). On a `full-v2` build
    there is no `Last-Event-ID` to send. Reconnect on `full-v2` therefore either resumes nothing or
    does not happen — decide which, and route it through a paired accessor with a constant
    `full-v2` answer rather than a second call-site `#[cfg]`.
  - Retry count and backoff are Claude's discretion (see below).

- **D-04: Bounded receive queue; the reader awaits capacity.** The channel is
  `mpsc::unbounded_channel()` today (`:598`), which was implicitly bounded by the whole-body
  collect. Once the stream never ends, an unbounded queue makes D-02's per-event bound a paper
  guarantee.
  - Real TCP backpressure: when the consumer is behind, the reader stops pulling and the server's
    window closes. **Never drops a frame** — which matters because this queue carries
    `sampling`/`roots`/`elicit` **requests**, and a dropped request strands a correlation until it
    times out, unlike a dropped progress notification.
  - *Deliberately NOT the server-side precedent.* `V2_PROGRESS_QUEUE_CAPACITY = 64` drop-newest is
    correct for progress frames and wrong here, for the reason above. The two directions are
    allowed to differ; say so in the code rather than letting a reader assume drift.
  - *Accepted risk:* a consumer that never drains wedges the stream. The reader is already its own
    spawned task, so backpressure lands on the reader, not the consumer.

- **D-05: A frame that fails to parse ENDS the stream with a named error.** Today
  `if let Ok(msg) = parse_message(..)` drops it silently (`:1051`). A frame the client cannot parse
  may be a server-to-client request, and swallowing it produces a hang with no signal at either
  end. Same treatment as parser overflow, so corruption has **one** story rather than two.

### The emitter

- **D-06: `RequestHandlerExtra` only — no trait changes anywhere.** `extra.log(..)`, mirroring
  `report_progress` (`src/server/cancellation.rs:657`). **No `PeerHandle` method.**
  - Criterion 3's `cargo semver-checks` verdict is therefore a clean *"no public trait surface
    touched"* rather than *"additive with a default body"*. External `PeerHandle` implementors are
    unaffected rather than defaulted-around.
  - *Note for the planner:* the roadmap's criterion 3 anticipated a defaulted `PeerHandle` method
    and justified the phase split partly on that basis. That justification does not evaporate — the
    client-transport work is reason enough — but do not add the trait method just because the
    roadmap's prose implies one.

- **D-07: The notification sink hoists to a shared per-request unit.** `ServerProgressReporter::new`
  takes `Arc<dyn Fn(Notification) + Send + Sync>` — a sink that already carries **any**
  `Notification`. It is already plumbed into both dispatch roots. But `progress_reporter_for`
  (`src/server/mod.rs:1314`) only builds it when `req._meta` carries a progress token, so today the
  sink is `None` for every call that did not ask for progress.
  - One function builds the sink for a request. The progress-token gate then applies **only** to the
    `ProgressReporter`; the log sink is unconditional.
  - **Both roots call the shared unit** — `ServerCore::handle_request` (`src/server/core.rs`) and
    `Server::handle_request_with_context` (`src/server/mod.rs`). This is the same pattern 118.1 used
    for `attach_request_peer`, which exists precisely because those twin roots drift.

- **D-08: No sink → `Ok(())`, silently dropped.** Matches `report_progress` exactly
  (`cancellation.rs:663`). Handlers stay callable outside a server — `RequestHandlerExtra::default()`
  is documented for "testing and simple tool invocations" — without special-casing.
  - *Accepted cost, stated plainly:* a misplumbed transport looks identical to a quiet handler. The
    conformance fence catches it (a test asserting logs arrive fails loudly when none do), so this
    is a production-diagnostics hole, not a false-green in the gate.

- **D-09: No rate limit on the emitter.** `ServerProgressReporter` rate-limits to 100ms, and that is
  right for progress: dropping an intermediate value loses nothing because the next supersedes it.
  Log records are not idempotent — **each one is the only copy of its information**, and a rate
  limiter silently deletes evidence. D-04's queue backpressure already bounds the downstream.

### Level filtering

- **D-10: Filter on both eras.** Honour v1's `logging/setLevel` and read v2's
  `io.modelcontextprotocol/logLevel` per-request `_meta` key.
  - `LOG_LEVEL_REQUEST_META_KEY` (`src/server/streamable_http_server.rs:1429`) exists and is
    referenced **only** by the `V2_RETIRED_METHODS` table (`:1485`). v2 today advertises a
    replacement mechanism that does nothing. Closing that is part of this decision.

- **D-11 — The v1 level lives in the existing `v1::V1State` session map.** Confirmed:
  `ServerState.server` is one `Arc<tokio::sync::Mutex<Server>>` shared by every session
  (`streamable_http_server.rs:479`). Putting the level there would let client B's `setLevel` change
  client A's filtering.
  - The per-session home already exists and is **already v1-gated**, which matches `setLevel`'s life
    exactly since v2 retired it. On a `full-v2` build `V1State` is a zero-sized twin, so those builds
    allocate nothing for a mechanism they do not have — structural, not a runtime branch someone can
    forget to take (SMPL-02 / D-03 / D-10 of the v1 pairing).
  - *Plumbing question left to research:* `logging/setLevel` arrives as a JSON-RPC request and the
    session identity is known at the HTTP layer. Whether the level is captured by intercepting at
    that layer (as `V2_RETIRED_METHODS` does) or by reaching session state through the
    `ProtocolContext` already threaded into dispatch is an implementation choice, not a locked one.

- **D-12: Default level is `info`.** Conventional across MCP implementations; `debug` and finer are
  suppressed until asked for, so a chatty handler cannot flood a client that never opted in. The
  conformance scenario emits at `info` or above and therefore passes unconfigured.

- **D-13: Both dispatch roots must agree about `setLevel`.** Today
  `Server::process_client_request` answers `Ok(json!({}))` for `SetLoggingLevel` with **no era
  branch anywhere in `src/`** — the v2 retirement is enforced only at the HTTP layer.
  `crates/pmcp-team-servers/tests/era_matrix.rs` flags this in its module doc: *"the REPLACEMENT
  mechanism works … only the retirement of the old RPC is missing."*
  - The `Server` arm stores-and-honours on v1 and retires on v2. 118.1 spent real effort collapsing
    exactly this class of divergence (`completion/complete` had two dispatchers giving two
    *different* wrong answers); leaving a known one open invites the next reader to trust the wrong
    root.
  - This arm is the `Subscribe | Unsubscribe | SetLoggingLevel | Ping` residual. **Only
    `SetLoggingLevel` is in scope** — do not unify the other three here.

### Proof and the gate

- **D-14: Hold the suite pin at `0.2.0-alpha.11`; re-pin as the FINAL task.** Repeats D-08 of Phase
  118.1 verbatim. Fix against the referee that produced 118.1's closing numbers so the delta is
  attributable to these two changes alone; then bump to whatever is newest, re-measure once, and
  report the bump's delta **separately**. Two clean numbers, not one confounded one.
  - 118.1's own bump delta was recorded as **nil** (the pin was held at plan 14), so 118.1's closing
    numbers *are* `0.2.0-alpha.11` numbers and the comparison is like-for-like.

- **D-15: Three fences — two independent, one joint.**
  1. `deprecated_capabilities_complete_under_both_eras`
     (`crates/pmcp-team-servers/tests/era_matrix.rs:626`) flips `no-live-stream` → `completed`.
     `DEP_STATUS_NO_LIVE_STREAM` (`:480`) and the module-doc paragraph at `:609-625` describing the
     client gap both need rewriting to describe the FIXED state, with the pre-fix state kept as
     history.
  2. The suite's `tools-call-with-logging` goes green on the `2025-11-25` leg
     (`GAP_ATTRIBUTABLE_FAILURES` → 0).
  3. **A new joint fence: pmcp's own client receives a `notifications/message` over a live stream.**
     This is the only test where pmcp is on **both** ends — every existing proof of the channel uses
     a raw TCP reader as the client.
  - **D-04 of Phase 118.1 carries forward:** each fence must be demonstrated **RED against the
    unfixed tree** before its fix lands.

- **D-16: Hard-require v1 exit 0 in the CI gate.** If the leg genuinely exits 0 after the emitter
  lands, gate on the exit code — a floor that only counts checks can be satisfied by a run that
  fails, and this is the first era leg that could carry the stronger guarantee.
  - **D-21 carries forward verbatim: no `--expected-failures`, no allowlist, no known-failure baseline.**
  - **⚠ Tension with D-14 the planner must sequence around:** the re-pin is the final task and a
    newer suite can introduce failures that red a gate hardened to `exit 0`. Harden the gate against
    the **held-pin** measurement, then treat the re-pin task's re-measurement as a gate on the gate —
    if the bump reds it, that is a reportable finding, not a reason to weaken D-16.
  - The refuted `ServerAcceptsWhitespaceHeaderValue` flake is the other live risk: if it is scored
    on a future pin, exit 0 becomes unattainable for a suite-side reason. Record that exposure in
    the phase summary rather than pre-emptively softening the gate.

- **D-17: Mint `CONF-09` (client SSE transport) and `CONF-10` (log emitter).** Both are
  conformance-driven and measured by the same suite, so they belong to the CONF- lineage. Two IDs,
  not one, so one residual can close while the other stays open.
  - `REQUIREMENTS.md` already carries a traceability warning — 10 REQ-IDs appear in bodies without
    table rows (`D-15`, `D-18`, `SEP-2106`, `PKG-01..04`, `PKGX-01/02`, `PKGR-01`). **Add table rows
    for CONF-09/CONF-10 at minting time** rather than widening that warning.

### Claude's Discretion

- Reconnect attempt count and backoff curve (D-03) — pick sane defaults; expose a knob only if the
  planner finds a caller that needs one.
- The bounded queue's capacity (D-04).
- `extra.log`'s exact signature: `LoggingLevel` enum vs convenience wrappers
  (`extra.info`/`warn`/`error`), what fills `LogMessageParams.logger` by default, and whether the
  payload is a `String` or the spec's arbitrary JSON `data`. Not discussed; research should pick.
- Whether the server must advertise `ServerCapabilities.logging` (`src/types/capabilities.rs:118`)
  for the emitter to fire, and whether the dual-conformance example's declaration needs updating.
  Not discussed.
- What a malformed `io.modelcontextprotocol/logLevel` value does — reject with `-32602`, or ignore
  and fall back to `info`. Not discussed.
- Wave/plan split across the two residuals, subject to the joint fence (D-15.3) needing both halves
  landed.

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets
- **`SseParser::feed`** — already incremental, already bounded, already hardened by Phase 113's
  CR-01/CR-02 round. The client is not missing a parser; it is missing a streaming read.
- **`Arc<dyn Fn(Notification) + Send + Sync>`** — the progress sink already carries *any*
  notification and is already plumbed into both dispatch roots. The emitter needs the same sink,
  un-gated from the progress token.
- **`ServerProgressReporter`** — the structural template for the emitter, minus the rate limiter
  (D-09) and minus the token gate (D-07).
- **`v1::V1State`** — the per-session home for the log level, already v1-gated and zero-sized on
  `full-v2`.
- **`apply_resumption_header` + `SseParser::last_event_id`** — everything reconnect-with-resumption
  needs on a `v1-compat` build already exists.
- **`assert_binary_is_not_stale`** — the staleness hazard that produced a false RED in 118.1 is now
  structural.

### Established Patterns
- **Shared unit called by both dispatch roots.** `attach_request_peer` (`src/server/core.rs`) is the
  living example, created in 118.1 for exactly this reason. D-07 follows it.
- **Additive-with-default trait methods for semver.** `PeerHandle::sample_with_tools` and
  `PeerHandle::elicit` both ship this way. D-06 declines to use it — that is a choice, not an
  oversight.
- **Fail loudly rather than synthesize.** `PeerHandle::elicit`'s default body returns
  `METHOD_NOT_FOUND` rather than a forged `ElicitAction`, on the stated grounds that a silent
  success forges the one signal the method exists for. D-05 applies the same reasoning to a corrupt
  frame.
- **Paired v1 modules with zero-sized `full-v2` twins.** A property of the type, not of a runtime
  branch. D-11 lands inside this pattern; D-03's reconnect must respect it rather than adding a
  second call-site `#[cfg]`.
- **Byte-literal fences derived from the spec, not from pmcp's own serializer.** 118.1 D-04.

### Integration Points
- `start_sse` and the POST-response read → the receive channel → the client's event loop.
- The per-request sink hoist touches **both** `ServerCore::handle_request` and
  `Server::handle_request_with_context`. Changing one and not the other is the phase's most likely
  silent defect.
- `logging/setLevel` handling spans the HTTP layer (session identity, v2 retirement) and the
  `Server` dispatcher (the residual arm). D-13 makes them agree.

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- The POST-stream deadlock (D-01) was **not** in the roadmap's scope statement. It surfaced during
  this discussion from reading `:1543`, and the developer widened scope deliberately to include it.
  It is the reason "GET stream only" was rejected.
- The distinction driving D-04 and D-09, stated once so it is not re-derived: **progress frames are
  supersedable, log records and back-channel requests are not.** Every place the server-side
  drop-newest precedent is declined, that is the reason.
- D-16 is the first time an era leg is gated on its **exit code** rather than a check-count floor.
  Treat that as the headline of the phase's gate work.

</specifics>

<deferred>
## Deferred Ideas

- **Cross-session `client_capabilities` misattribution.** `ServerState.server` is one
  `Arc<Mutex<Server>>` shared across every StreamableHTTP session, and `handle_initialize` writes
  capabilities to that server-level lock — so client A's handler can read client B's capabilities.
  Found by the `/code-review` pass over Phase 118.1 (2026-08-11) and **currently unowned**. Offered
  as a fold-in here (same per-session storage mechanism as D-11, same seam open) and **declined** to
  keep this phase to its two stated residuals. Before G-9 the accessor was permanently `None` on v1
  — wrong but honest; it now returns confidently wrong cross-session data, and a handler gating
  `peer.sample()` on it will make the wrong call. **Needs a phase.**
- **`route_to_session_stream` swallows a send failure**, parking a handler for 30s while holding the
  server mutex. Reported by the same review, not fixed.
- **The other three arms of the `Subscribe | Unsubscribe | SetLoggingLevel | Ping` residual** —
  D-13 unifies only `SetLoggingLevel`. The twin-root divergence for the other three remains.
- **`extra.log` with no sink is silently `Ok(())`** (D-08). Accepted, but it is a production
  diagnostics hole; a `tracing::warn!`-once-per-handle variant was considered and can be revisited.

</deferred>
