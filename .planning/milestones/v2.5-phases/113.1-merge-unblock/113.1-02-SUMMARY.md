# Plan 113.1-02 — Summary

Closed **D-113-R**: `SseParser::drain_complete_lines` restarted its `find('\n')` search at offset
0 on every `feed()` call, so a peer choosing 1-byte chunk framing got one full-buffer rescan per
byte. This is HTTP-09's clause 3 — *"no scan over peer-chosen input is worse than O(n)"* — and a
remote CPU-exhaustion channel, not a tidiness issue.

**Every guard here was demonstrated falsifiable TWICE**: RED against the unfixed tree before the
fix, and RED again under a post-fix negative control reproducible from the committed tree.

## 2. The fix — two edits, deliberately atomic

RESEARCH § Pitfall 1 measured why they cannot be separated, and Phase C's numbers confirm it: with
the `debug_assert` already gone but the cursor reverted, growth is still 14.85x. The assertion alone
was not the problem, and the cursor alone would not have shown green.

**Edit 1 — the scan-window cursor (D-12).** `let scan_start = self.buffer.len();` captured
immediately *before* `self.buffer.push_str(data);`, and `search_from` starting at `scan_start`
instead of at 0.

`consumed` still starts at **0**, and that is load-bearing rather than incidental. It drives both
the final `drain(..consumed)` and the CRLF lookback guard: when the retained prefix ends with `\r`
and the new chunk begins with `\n`, `line_end == scan_start` and `line_end - 1` points INTO the
retained prefix. The guard `line_end > consumed` is then `scan_start > 0` — true, so the `\r` is
correctly trimmed. Seeding `consumed` to `scan_start` would make that guard false and leak the `\r`
into the line. The reasoning is written into the code comment, not just here.

Unchanged and asserted so: `line_end` remains a BYTE index; the CRLF lookback stays
`self.buffer.as_bytes()[line_end - 1] == b'\r'` and never `chars().nth(...)` (that was T-113-67's
remote panic); the guard stays `line_end > consumed`; the final `drain(..consumed)` is untouched.

**Edit 2 — delete the per-call `debug_assert` (D-15).** `debug_assert!(!self.buffer.contains('\n'))`
was itself an O(retained-length) scan on every call. It is replaced by a prose comment stating (a)
the invariant, (b) why it is now the *soundness precondition* for `scan_start`, (c) why it was
deleted rather than kept, and (d) the name of its replacement pin. Neither `#[cfg]`-gating it nor
moving the perf tests to `--release` was taken — D-15 rejects both, and release hides the defect
entirely.

The existing "LINEAR, not quadratic" comment was extended to distinguish the **per-LINE** quadratic
fixed in `0493d9fb` from the **per-CALL** quadratic fixed here, so a future reader cannot conflate
them.

## 3. The four guards

| Guard | Kind | Phase A | Committed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sse_parser_feed_1b_chunks_stays_within_its_linear_time_budget` | unit (perf, absolute) | RED ✅ | GREEN |
| `sse_parser_feed_cost_grows_linearly_not_quadratically` | unit (perf, ratio, machine-independent) | RED ✅ | GREEN |
| `property_feed_chunking_invariance` | property | GREEN (as designed) | GREEN, 1000 cases |
| `drain_leaves_no_newline_in_the_retained_buffer` | unit | GREEN (as designed) | GREEN |

Guards 3 and 4 are correctness properties, not timing ones, so they are green on both trees by
design — guard 3 exists to catch the specific way the D-12 optimisation could go wrong, and guard 4
is the pin that replaces the deleted assertion.

**Guard 3's claim is stated precisely.** It asserts *arbitrary text under arbitrary
character-boundary chunking*, and says so in its name and rustdoc — not a claim over arbitrary raw
bytes. `feed` takes `&str`, and every `&str` is valid UTF-8 by construction, so a `feed` call can
never receive half a character: byte-level fragmentation is **unrepresentable** here, not merely
untested. The rustdoc says where that coverage actually lives
(`take_utf8_prefix`'s `property_take_utf8_prefix_retains_at_most_a_three_byte_tail`) so no reader
concludes the property has a hole. Splitting B is built from character counts via a `Vec<char>`,
never from raw byte offsets.

**Guard 4 covers feeds of every shape**, including the one the plan called out as impossible and
which is therefore *not* tested as stated: a chunk boundary immediately before and after a
multi-byte character (reachable), rather than a multi-byte character split across two feeds
(unrepresentable). Cases: mid-line split; CRLF split across a chunk boundary; a bare empty line; a
chunk that is exactly one `\n`; a boundary hugging a multi-byte character; a lone CR retained with
no LF yet; and nothing but a partial line.
