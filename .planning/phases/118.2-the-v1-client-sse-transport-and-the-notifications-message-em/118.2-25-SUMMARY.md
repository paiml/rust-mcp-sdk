## What a consumer holding two clones of one transport observed BEFORE, and observes AFTER

For plan 24's ledger, stated as a comparison rather than as an absence:

> **Before**, a consumer holding two clones of one `StreamableHttpTransport` — the durable-agent shape, in which a single transport is shared across concurrent tasks — had two live hazards that only the pmcp `Client`'s outer transport write guard was accidentally suppressing. First, when both clones' POSTs were answered `401` at overlapping times, **both** called `AuthProvider::on_unauthorized()` and, one step later, **both** reached `get_access_token()` concurrently against the cache the first had just emptied. Against a rotating refresh token that means two presentations of the same refresh token: the identity provider accepts one and rejects the other, and the rejected caller's purge destroys the token the winner had just cached — so the transport's auth fails permanently until an out-of-band re-authentication. This is the recorded SDK blocker for the durable-agent deployment shape, not a thought experiment. Second, when both clones restarted the session stream at overlapping times, each took a `None` abort handle, each opened a GET, and the second's store orphaned the first's reader; because `close()` aborts exactly one `JoinHandle`, that orphan outlived the transport holding a live peer-controlled socket nothing could reach.
>
> **After**, a concurrent `401` produces exactly one `on_unauthorized` call and — for an `AuthProvider` that caches what it vends, which is what the trait's own rustdoc directs implementors to do — exactly one vend; the loser observes that its token generation was already superseded, skips the purge, and takes its retry token from the cache the winner warmed. Both callers still succeed; neither is starved. A concurrent restart produces exactly one live reader, and `close()` reaches it. The retry POST is **not** serialised, so the transport gains no whole-transport bottleneck. A caller that uses a transport one call at a time sees byte-identical behaviour: the same single 401 retry, the same single session-stream restart, the same errors. No public signature changed, no trait changed, no dependency was added, and no bound, deadline or timeout was introduced anywhere.
>
> **What remains open, deliberately and with an owner rather than as a review gap:** the "exactly one vend" guarantee is PRECONDITIONED on the `AuthProvider` implementation caching what its `get_access_token` vends before returning. Nothing in the trait requires that — `on_unauthorized` defaults to a no-op and `get_access_token` is unconstrained — so this is an assumption about downstream code, not an invariant this crate enforces. Against a NON-caching provider the generation check is inert, the loser vends too, and all the lock buys is that the two vends are SERIALISED rather than simultaneous, which a rotating refresh token still rejects. That residual **cannot be fenced in-repo**: there is no in-tree client-side `AuthProvider` implementation at all, only test doubles. It is stated in `refresh_lock`'s rustdoc and is disclosed by plan 24, not closed here — closing it would mean adding a caching requirement to a public trait, which is a new contract on every external implementor and belongs to a decision rather than to a gap-closure round.

## Decisions Made

See `key-decisions` in the frontmatter. Two worth restating inline:

**The fence was written so that the wrong fix cannot satisfy it, and that was then MEASURED.** The five assertions are ordered so the vend peak fires first; the doc comment states in its own words that the two purge assertions are insufficient alone and why; and the counterfactual probe above proves the claim rather than asserting it. This is the fourth round in this phase in which a fence was at risk of measuring the operation adjacent to the one that breaks.

**Both gates release on a count OR a bounded grace, never on a count alone.** A pure "wait for two arrivals" barrier deadlocks the *fixed* tree by construction — there the second operation cannot begin until the first has finished — so a fence built that way would report the fix as a hang. The OR makes both trees terminate and moves the assertion onto the END state. That rationale is written at both gates, because it is the kind of thing a later simplification removes.
